# Vedic Astrology's Relationship with Western Astrology

When comparing Vedic Astrology and Western Astrology, it becomes clear that both systems possess unique strengths and valuable perspectives.

Western Astrology generally places greater emphasis on psychology and the workings of the mind. Because of this, a Western birth chart can serve as an excellent tool for counselling and self-understanding. For those seeking insight into their thoughts, emotions, personality, and inner psychological patterns, Western Astrology offers significant value.

Vedic Astrology, by contrast, places greater emphasis on **karma** and the timing of karmic results. Its primary focus is to identify which karmas are likely to manifest during an individual's lifetime and when those effects are most likely to occur.

One of the distinctive features of Jyotish is that it not only predicts karmic outcomes but also prescribes **remedial measures** that can help reduce or soften the impact of challenging karmas.

For this reason, Vedic Astrology is both **predictive and preventive**, offering practical solutions to many of life's challenges.

# Vedic Astrology's Predictive Capabilities

One of the most widely practiced branches of Vedic Astrology involves predicting the karmic influences that an individual may experience throughout life. This information is derived by interpreting the planetary configurations present within a person's Jyotish birth chart.

Jyotish can forecast both opportunities and obstacles across all major areas of life, including health, home, finances, career, relationships, children, and spiritual development.[1] Equally important, it can indicate *when* these events are likely to unfold.[2] This is accomplished through the unique Vedic system of planetary periods.

# Vedic Astrology's Preventive Capabilities

Jyotish is not limited to prediction alone. It also serves a preventive function by offering practical methods for modifying the effects of returning karmas. These methods are commonly known as **Planetary Antidotes** or **Remedial Measures**.

In this way, Vedic Astrology provides a powerful system of karmic balancing and correction. It is truly a form of **karmic astrology**.

Through the insights provided by Jyotish, potential difficulties resulting from returning karmas can first be identified and then reduced, managed, or in some cases completely avoided. Likewise, highly beneficial periods can be anticipated, allowing individuals to make the most of favorable circumstances.

The remedial measures recommended within Jyotish help correct planetary imbalances reflected in the birth chart. By addressing these imbalances, it becomes possible to minimize challenges that may otherwise arise in the future. Such remedies may include wearing prescribed gemstones, performing charitable acts, reciting specific mantras, or participating in Vedic ceremonies such as **Homas**, **Yagnas**, or **Yajnas**.

Jyotish Astrology – Quick Facts
Benefits of Jyotish
Jyotish Astrology is a form of Karmic Astrology. Its primary purpose is to forecast our returning karmas—whether favourable or challenging—and indicate how and when they are likely to manifest in our lives.
It helps identify the blessings and opportunities we are destined to receive, as well as the obstacles and difficulties we may face. With this knowledge, we can make the most of beneficial periods while exercising greater awareness and caution during less favourable times.
Through an analysis of the planetary configuration at birth, Jyotish provides timely, high-probability predictions across the major areas of life related to Artha, Dharma, Kama, and Moksha. In practical terms, these include health, wealth, family, children, success, career, social status, love, relationships, personal development, and spiritual growth.
Vedic Astrology also reveals the specific periods in life when the “cosmic postman” delivers our karmic results and experiences.
In addition to being predictive, Vedic Astrology is also preventive in nature. Jyotish offers a wide range of remedial measures designed to lessen the impact of returning negative karmas, helping to make life smoother and more comfortable.
Jyotish can also be applied to determine the most favourable timing for important actions, reflecting the principle that “there is a time for every purpose under heaven.” It can therefore indicate the best time to marry, launch a business, begin constructing a house, and undertake other significant ventures. As the saying goes, “well begun is half done.”
Key Differences Between Vedic and Western Astrology
Jyotish places greater emphasis on predicting returning karmas, whereas Western Astrology tends to focus more on psychological understanding and personal analysis. As a result, Jyotish is particularly effective for addressing practical life situations, while Western Astrology often serves as a useful foundation for counselling and self-reflection.
Vedic Astrology arises from a tradition that sees the cosmos and all living beings as parts of a unified whole. Accordingly, the planetary arrangement at the moment of birth is viewed as a highly concentrated and coded source of information. A skilled Vedic Astrologer can interpret and decode this information to provide time-sensitive, high-probability predictions.
Jyotish works exclusively with 9 celestial bodies or “planets.” The placement of these 9 planets within the 12 signs, Houses, and 27 Nakshatras (constellations or lunar mansions) contains all the information required for accurate prediction. Because of this, Jyotish does not rely on planets beyond Saturn, such as Neptune, Uranus, or Pluto, which play a role in Western Astrology.
Therefore, Jyotish deals directly with an underlying field of information rather than with “planetary forces,” which is a more common perspective in Western Astrology. As astronomers discover additional planets and planet-like objects, Western Astrology continues to evolve. Vedic Astrology, however, remains unchanged in this regard.
Unlike modern Western Astrology, which uses the movable Tropical Zodiac, Vedic Astrology is based on the fixed Sidereal Zodiac, which has maintained its position for thousands of years. While Western astrological rules have gradually evolved over time, the principles of Jyotish have remained remarkably consistent.
Origins of Vedic Astrology
Jyotish is one of the ancient sciences associated with yoga, all of which originate from the same Vedic tradition. Rather than being gradually developed or discovered over time, Jyotish was cognised by ancient seers in a distant age.
It has successfully stood the test of time, providing guidance and benefits to countless people over many thousands of years.

Vedic Astrology Explained: Jyotish
Soon after beginning a regular yoga practice, many people notice a deeper sense of connection with the plants, animals, and people around them. As we progress further along the path of yoga and meditation, we often become aware not only of our connection with the Earth, but also of our relationship with the wider universe. With expanded awareness, we may even experience a glimpse of the “unity of all things” spoken of by advanced yogis.
Interestingly, modern science also recognises this connection. Scientists tell us that the iron flowing through our blood and the calcium within our bones were not present at the beginning of the universe. These elements were formed later in the stellar furnaces of stars billions of years ago. In a very real sense, science confirms that we are indeed “stardust.”
Ancient Vedic sages from the yogic tradition understood the interconnected nature of existence and recognised how life on Earth is influenced by the stars. From this understanding emerged another of yoga’s sister sciences—the ancient system of Vedic astrology known as Jyotish (pronounced “Joe-tish”). The word Jyotish is derived from the Sanskrit word Jyoti, meaning “light.” Jyotish therefore shines a light on our past, our present, and most importantly, our future. Like yoga itself, Vedic astrology aims to help us live healthier, happier, and more fulfilling lives.
There are three principal areas in which Jyotish can be applied. The most common application involves examining the planetary arrangement at the moment of birth. From the distribution of the nine planets among the twelve zodiac signs, a skilled Vedic astrologer can decode and predict our returning karmas—whether favourable or challenging. These predictions cover all the major areas of life, including health, wealth, family, children, status, career, relationships, and spiritual development. In the hands of an experienced astrologer, Jyotish can often provide a karmic perspective on health issues that may otherwise seem difficult to explain.
As well as being predictive, Jyotish is also preventive. Having identified potential difficulties ahead, it can recommend remedies designed to minimise their effects. As one enlightened yogi expressed it, these remedies help “avert the danger that has not yet arisen.” In this way, Jyotish offers the possibility of modifying the impact of returning karmas. Just as the modern world uses software engineering to solve technical problems, the ancient world provided us with what might be called karmic engineering to address life's challenges.
If imbalances in planetary energies are identified within a birth chart, Jyotish offers a variety of techniques to restore harmony. These methods are known as remedial measures or planetary antidotes. One simple example is the use of gemstones that help balance the energy of a specific planet. Emeralds, for instance, are associated with balancing the energies of Mercury. To be effective, the gemstone should touch the skin and is therefore often worn as a pendant or mounted in a specially designed ring.
This approach differs significantly from the concept of birthstones found in Western astrology, as recommendations are based on a detailed analysis of the individual's birth chart. Other balancing methods may include the chanting of mantras or the performance of specific charitable acts. Many people who have applied Jyotish remedies report the removal of significant obstacles and difficulties. While not all karmas can be entirely eliminated, their effects can often be reduced considerably.
There is a well-known saying: “There is a time for every purpose under heaven.” Jyotish astrology helps determine the most favourable time for that purpose, whether it involves marriage, building a new home, making a major investment, launching a business, or undertaking another important venture. By selecting the right time to begin a significant activity, we align ourselves with the supportive forces of nature, encouraging positive growth and successful outcomes. As an enlightened yogi once remarked, “Well begun is half done.”
Jyotish can also assess how well individuals are likely to work together in harmony. This is determined through an analysis of the planetary positions in their birth charts. Traditionally, compatibility analysis has been widely used in India to evaluate marriage partnerships and help ensure harmonious relationships. However, the same principles can also be applied successfully to business partnerships and other forms of collaboration.
It is important to recognise, however, that not every challenge we face is the direct result of returning karma. Some difficulties may arise from our current environment or lifestyle choices. In such cases, the application of other yoga-related Vedic sciences, such as Vastu (the yogic science of harmonious living spaces) and Ayurveda (the yogic system of healthcare), can often help address these issues effectively.
Jyotish comes from a tradition that holds the view that the Creator does not play dice. According to this perspective, we are not born at a specific moment, under a particular arrangement of planets, by mere chance. Rather, the planetary indicators present at birth reflect how karmas from previous lifetimes are likely to unfold in the current incarnation. From this standpoint, we are not controlled by the planets. They simply act as the “cosmic postman,” delivering the karmic packages addressed to us.
Some people prefer not to know what the future may hold. However, the forecasts provided by Vedic astrology can be viewed in much the same way as a weather forecast. If rain is expected, we take an umbrella. Likewise, if Jyotish indicates potential challenges on the horizon, we can proceed with greater awareness and caution while remembering that difficult periods will eventually pass. Like weather forecasting, Jyotish deals with probabilities rather than absolute certainties.
It may seem remarkable that predictions can be made entirely from the planetary arrangement contained within a birth chart—the “planetary code.” Yet we readily accept that a scientist can study the genetic code within a seed and predict how it will develop. The information contained within the seed’s DNA exists in a highly condensed form. Similarly, the planetary arrangement at birth contains encoded information that a Vedic astrologer can interpret to understand the likely unfolding of life events.
Some individuals may find it difficult to accept that certain aspects of life could be predetermined rather than entirely governed by free will. An enlightened Vedic master once explained that life is actually a combination of both. He offered a simple illustration: “Even though our height as an adult is predetermined, the size of our waist is largely a matter of our own free will.”

Jyotish also possesses a unique predictive system that identifies the major periods of karmic influence throughout life. What, then, are the origins of Jyotish?
Like Yoga and Ayurveda, Jyotish belongs to the great family of Vedic sciences that originated through revealed knowledge or cognition within the consciousness of highly evolved sages, seers, and rishis of ancient times. A modern analogy might be that of connecting a computer to a vast remote server and downloading the required information. Of course, the sages required the correct “passwords”—mantras—to gain access to the knowledge stored within the cosmic intelligence.

To him, the cosmos was an intelligently organised and interconnected whole, where future events could be understood through the arrangement of the stars.
Jyotish has certainly stood the test of time. For more than five thousand years, people have derived guidance and benefits from this ancient science.
The next time we practise yoga outdoors on a warm summer evening, it is worth pausing to look up at the stars. Vedic Astrology FAQs 1
Summary: Introductory FAQs on Jyotish (Yogic or Vedic Astrology), including its connection with Yoga, the benefits of a Jyotish birth-chart interpretation, the key differences between Vedic and Western Astrology, the karmic implications and predictive capabilities of Jyotish, and Vedic Astrology’s unique system of remedial measures.
Q. What exactly is Vedic Astrology?
A. Vedic Astrology, also known as Jyotish or Yogic Astrology, is an ancient astrological system that is both predictive and preventive. It provides high-probability [1] predictions about our returning karmas and their likely effects during this lifetime. It also contains unique methods for forecasting when these karmic influences are most likely to manifest.
Vedic Astrology comes from a tradition that seeks to maximise individual evolution and progress. It includes a variety of remedial measures, often referred to as Planetary Antidotes, which are designed to counteract returning negative karmas and help re-balance the planetary influences reflected in the birth-chart. In this sense, Vedic Astrology can be viewed as the ultimate form of karmic engineering.
Q. Is Jyotish Astrology in any way linked to Yoga?
A. Yes. Both Yoga and Jyotish arise from the same ancient Vedic tradition. Over thousands of years, both disciplines have continued to bring benefits to generation after generation.
Jyotish (Vedic Astrology) is regarded as one of Yoga’s sister sciences, alongside Ayurveda (Yogic healthcare), Vastu (Yogic design of living spaces), and Pranayama (Yogic breathing practices). Together, these subjects represent important Vedic technologies that are particularly valuable in today’s fast-paced and stress-filled world.
Q. What benefits could we expect from a Vedic astrological chart interpretation?
A. There are several levels at which this question can be answered.
On a practical level, Vedic Astrology can provide guidance that improves day-to-day happiness and supports smoother progress through life. A birth-chart can offer insights into health, career, relationships, family, children, finances, happiness, good fortune, spiritual development, peace of mind, and even enlightenment. These areas correspond to the Vedic concepts of Artha, Dharma, Kama, and Moksha [2].
On another level, when we are seeking answers to challenges in specific areas of life, a Vedic perspective can be particularly helpful. It often places events within a broader cosmic framework and reminds us that many difficulties are temporary and time-dependent.
Following a consultation, a Jyotish Astrologer will usually recommend remedial measures to help balance adverse planetary influences. By following these recommendations—such as wearing an appropriate gemstone, which is very different from the concept of a Western birthstone—many people report feeling lighter, encountering fewer obstacles, and experiencing a greater sense of flow in life.
At an even deeper level, the ultimate purpose of Vedic Astrology is to help us recognise that we are not isolated individuals existing in a vast universe. Rather, we are intimately connected with the very fabric of creation itself. We are not only in the universe—the universe is also within us.
Q. What are the main differences between Vedic Astrology and Western Astrology?
A. Both systems have their own strengths and areas of emphasis.
Western Astrology tends to focus more on psychology, personality, and the workings of the mind. As a result, a Western birth-chart can be an excellent starting point for counselling and self-understanding. For those seeking insights into their emotions, thoughts, and psychological nature, Western Astrology has much to offer.
Vedic Astrology, by contrast, focuses more directly on karma and the prediction of returning karmas. It seeks to identify which karmic influences are likely to manifest during a lifetime and when they are most likely to occur. In addition, it provides remedial measures that can significantly reduce the effects of returning negative karmas. This makes Vedic Astrology both predictive and preventive.
The two systems also differ significantly in their methods and underlying concepts. Vedic Astrology uses the fixed or Sidereal Zodiac, while Western Astrology generally uses the Tropical Zodiac. Jyotish works with only the nine planets up to Saturn and employs unique predictive systems such as Mahadashas, which are considered far more important than transits. The Ascendant plays a central role in Jyotish, whereas the Sun sign carries much less importance than it does in Western Astrology.
It is also important to remember that not every difficulty we experience is the result of returning karma. Some problems may arise from our current environment or lifestyle. In such situations, the application of the other yoga-related Vedic sciences—particularly Vastu and Ayurveda—can often provide valuable solutions.
Q. How is it possible to make future, life-long predictions from just the planetary arrangements at the time of an individual’s birth?
A. We know that an acorn grows into an oak tree. It will not become a sycamore or a fir tree. Within the acorn exists all the information necessary to structure and guide the growth of the oak.
We cannot know with certainty how many branches the tree will have or exactly how large it will grow because environmental influences also play a role. Nevertheless, the essential blueprint of the tree is encoded within its DNA.
In a similar way, the probable effects of our returning karmas—which unfold through the sequential operation of the laws of nature—are reflected in the code contained within the planetary arrangement of the birth-chart. This implies that our birth did not occur at a random moment by chance. Instead, it reflects the Vedic understanding of the deep interconnectedness of life, consciousness, and matter throughout the universe.
Q. What are the ‘remedial measures’ mentioned in Jyotish?
A. The Vedic tradition holds that people should live healthy, happy, and fulfilled lives. For this reason, Vedic Astrology includes a wide range of remedial measures designed to reduce the impact of returning negative karmas and restore greater balance and harmony.
Because we are part of an integrated whole, certain actions can help modify karmic influences. These may include participation in Vedic ceremonies such as Yagnas or Yagyas, performing prescribed acts of charity, reciting specific Sanskrit mantras, or wearing suitable gemstones. Such measures can often modify, reduce, or deflect returning karmas to a significant extent, helping us avoid some of their more difficult consequences in this lifetime.
In this way, Jyotish enables us not only to detect returning karmas but also to help deflect them. For further information, see the article on Planetary Antidotes (Upayes).
Q. Can we change our returning karmas without recourse to the remedial measures of Jyotish?
A. Yes, this is certainly possible.
It is often said that the grace of the Guru can significantly modify returning karmas. Anyone fortunate enough to receive the grace of an enlightened spiritual master should consider themselves truly blessed.
Regular spiritual practices, particularly meditation, can also have a profound effect on karma. Many Jyotish astrologers have observed that predicted negative karmas often appear greatly reduced in the lives of long-term meditators. Vedic mantra-based meditation practices are considered especially effective in this regard.
Acts of charity are also said to be powerful tools for modifying certain karmic influences. One enlightened Vedic scholar suggested that people are more likely to retain and protect their hard-earned wealth if they regularly donate a small percentage of it to charitable causes each month.
In addition, helping and serving others through the practice of Seva is regarded as highly beneficial, both spiritually and karmically.

Vedic Astrology FAQs 2
Summary: Vedic Astrology (Jyotish) basic FAQs, including questions about its origins, its relationship with Hindu beliefs, Jyotish as an “applied” Vedic science, its use of the sidereal zodiac, and why it works with only the nine ‘planets’ up to and including Saturn.
Q. What are the origins of Vedic Astrology?
A. Vedic Astrology was cognized more than 5,000 years ago by an ancient, fully enlightened sage known as Maharishi Parashara [1]. He is said to have discovered the mathematical and astronomical principles that enable future trends to be predicted and potential difficulties to be avoided. This ancient yet timeless wisdom has successfully endured through the ages. Its techniques have been validated through extensive use and observation across many generations.
The teachings of Maharishi Parashara remain the primary foundation for modern Vedic Astrologers.
Parashara was a highly evolved soul whose elevated consciousness was fully awakened and aware of both its own nature and the dynamics governing evolution. To him, the cosmos functioned as an intelligently ordered and interconnected whole, where one event naturally led to another and the unfolding of life could therefore be understood and predicted. Vedic Astrology thus illustrates the relationship between the individual and the cosmic totality, linking human life with the universe itself.
Q. As Vedic Astrology comes from India, is it connected to the Hindu religion?
A. A similar question could be asked about Yoga, which also originated in India. Most people recognize that one does not need to hold Hindu beliefs to benefit from practicing Yoga. The same principle applies to all Vedic Sciences, including Jyotish, Ayurveda, and Vastu.
The Vedic Sciences are intended for the benefit of all people, irrespective of race, religion, nationality, or personal belief system. This ancient body of knowledge is universal and remains highly relevant in the modern world.
Q. Is Vedic Astrology a science?
A. Vedic Astrology is certainly highly mathematical, logical, and systematic in the way it applies the rules and principles of Jyotish to chart interpretation and prediction.
Since Jyotish views the universe as a field of energy and information, its perspective is broadly consistent with many modern scientific ideas concerning the nature of reality. Although thousands of years old, several of its concepts show interesting parallels with contemporary neuroscience, cosmology, and particle physics.
Vedic Astrology functions much like a long-range weather forecasting system and is therefore essentially probabilistic in nature. It seeks to identify when and how actions performed in the past may return as present or future influences in our lives. If potential “storms” are visible on the horizon, appropriate steps can be taken to minimize or avoid difficulties before they manifest. Likewise, when “fine weather” is indicated, we can move forward more effectively and fulfil our goals with greater ease.
However, Vedic Astrology is not knowledge derived through experimental investigation. Rather, it is regarded as cognized and revealed wisdom, provided for the benefit of humanity. This wisdom was revealed to a great Sage more than 5,000 years ago. The ancient Sanskrit texts form part of India's Vedic tradition, which also includes the sciences of Yoga and Ayurveda. Today, there is a growing revival of interest in this timeless knowledge.
Q. When I had my Jyotish chart prepared, I noticed that both my Sun and Ascendant were in different astrological signs compared with my Western Astrology chart. How is this possible?
A. Vedic Astrology divides the celestial sphere into the same thirty-degree segments used in Western Astrology, and these segments carry the same names, such as Aries, Taurus, and so on. However, there is one important difference that accounts for the variation in planetary positions between the two systems: the starting point of the zodiac.
The Sidereal Zodiac used in Vedic Astrology remains fixed relative to the distant background stars. As a result, the segment called Aries always occupies the same position in relation to the fixed stars and constellations. In Jyotish, the beginning of Aries is always referenced to a fixed stellar point.
Western Astrology, on the other hand, uses the Tropical Zodiac, whose starting point is based on the position of the spring equinox. This equinox point gradually shifts relative to the fixed stars. Although the movement is small from year to year, over centuries it becomes significant.
The starting points of the two zodiacs coincided approximately 1,700 years ago. Today, because of an astronomical phenomenon known as the precession of the equinoxes, they differ by roughly 24 degrees. This difference, known as the Ayanamsha, causes many planetary positions to appear in different signs when comparing the two systems [2].
Of course, if you had been born around 300 AD, there would have been virtually no difference between them.
Q. Why does Vedic Astrology use only the nine ‘planets’ up to and including Saturn? Surely the planets beyond Saturn also influence our lives?
A. In Vedic Astrology, all the information required for prediction across every area of life is encoded within the arrangement of the nine ‘Grahas’ [3] at the moment of birth.
Jyotish does not view planets as emitting some form of physical force that directly acts upon individuals. Instead, the emphasis is on encoded information. This is not a mechanistic, Newtonian concept of force. Rather, Vedic Astrology regards the universe and all beings within it as components of an interconnected field of energy, order, and intelligence.
The placement of the nine planets within the twelve zodiac signs, twelve houses, and twenty-seven Nakshatras at birth reflects how life will unfold over time as a consequence of past actions. Therefore, Jyotish deals with coded karmic information, which contains all the information necessary for interpretation and prediction, rather than with planetary forces themselves.

Vedic Astrology FAQs 3
Summary: Further FAQs on Jyotish Astrology, including questions about ‘Sun signs’, compatibility analysis between partners, determining the most auspicious time for new beginnings such as marriage (Muhurta), and the use of ‘birthstones’.
Q. Does Vedic Astrology use ‘Sun signs’?
A. No. In Jyotish, our ‘Sun sign’ is considered a relatively minor influence. Much greater importance is placed on factors such as the ascendant, the strength and placement of the planets, the Moon sign, and the lunar constellations or Nakshatras.
On a personal note, I find ‘Sun signs’ great fun (for example: me – Aries, my wife – Scorpio!). However, even Western Astrologers regard Sun signs as only one small part of a much broader and more detailed analysis. Therefore, simple ‘Sun sign’ astrology, especially the type commonly found in newspapers and magazines, should be viewed as entertainment rather than serious applied astrology capable of providing meaningful guidance.
It is also worth remembering that a person’s ‘Sun sign’ in Jyotish may differ from their Sun sign in Western Astrology because the Vedic system uses the sidereal zodiac.
Q. Can Vedic Astrology provide guidance on the compatibility of marriage partners?
A. Yes, it certainly can. Jyotish is capable of offering reliable compatibility assessments for people involved in a wide range of relationships, including marriage, business partnerships, and other forms of association. It is important to understand that ‘compatibility’ means the individuals are likely to work well together with fewer disagreements or tensions. In the context of marriage, compatibility does not necessarily indicate that a couple will be in love or find one another physically attractive.
To conduct a compatibility analysis, a Jyotish Astrologer must first prepare the birth charts of both individuals. This requires details such as the place of birth, date of birth, and, most importantly, an accurate birth time for each person.
Alongside the main birth charts—where factors such as the ascendant, 7th House, Mars placements, and other key indicators are examined—the astrologer will usually analyse the 9th divisional chart (Navamsha) and the birth Nakshatras (lunar mansions). In some cases, a numerical scoring system may also be employed. Such analysis requires considerable astrological knowledge and expertise.
As with most matters in Jyotish, situations are rarely entirely positive or entirely negative. Nevertheless, a skilled Jyotish Astrologer should be able to provide sound advice regarding a couple’s ability to live harmoniously, establish a home, raise children, manage a business together, and cooperate successfully in other areas of life.
One final point: if a couple are already married, it is generally considered unethical to request a compatibility analysis from a Jyotish Astrologer. In such circumstances, the services of a marriage guidance counsellor would be far more appropriate.
Q. Can Jyotish Astrology tell me the best time and date on which to get married?
A. You may be familiar with the saying, “there is a time for every purpose under heaven.” The good news is that Jyotish can identify highly favourable dates and times for beginning many important activities. These may include getting married, launching a new business, making long-term investments, laying the foundation stone of a new home, and much more.
Beginning an activity at the most auspicious time ensures that all the ‘forces of nature’ are supporting the intended outcome. Choosing the right moment can also reduce obstacles and make progress smoother and more effortless. The enlightened Master Maharishi Mahesh Yogi [1] often said: “Well begun is half-done!”
This specialised branch of Vedic Astrology is known as finding the best Muhurta (an auspicious time period). It follows its own set of rules and principles and requires additional skills and knowledge beyond standard birth-chart interpretation.
Determining the most suitable Muhurta involves analysing combinations of factors such as the day of the week, lunar days (tithis), the Nakshatra (lunar mansion or constellation) occupied by the Moon, the time of day, and many other considerations. These factors must then be assessed in relation to the individual’s birth chart, particularly their birth Nakshatra or ‘birth star’.
The process also includes examining current planetary positions, avoiding periods when the Moon transits regions associated with eclipses, and considering numerous other astrological influences. This involves substantial work on the part of the astrologer, who must balance all these factors alongside the specific requirements of the event being planned. The result is a selection of dates, days, and time periods that are considerably more favourable for achieving a successful outcome.
On a personal note, I used this process to determine the ideal date, day, and time for my own marriage over 35 years ago. It appears to have worked rather well!
Q. I’ve heard that there is something called ‘Medical Astrology’. Is this for real?
A. Yes, Medical Astrology does exist. However, if you have a health concern, it is always preferable to seek advice first from a qualified Doctor—whether trained in Western medicine or Ayurveda—rather than from an Astrologer.
At a basic level, Jyotish can indicate possible karmic health issues carried forward from previous lifetimes. It is important to note that it deals only with karma brought into this life and not with health influences arising from current environmental factors, lifestyle choices, diet, or similar circumstances. By identifying imbalances within the birth chart, Jyotish may recommend a variety of remedial measures, often referred to as ‘planetary antidotes’, designed to help rebalance planetary influences.
At a more advanced level, Medical Astrology can examine the relationship between planetary influences and the Ayurvedic doshas. Here, Ayurveda and Jyotish complement each other and can work synergistically. In some cases, Jyotish may provide valuable insight into long-standing health conditions or illnesses that are difficult to diagnose. The process works as follows:
At the basic level, Vedic Astrology assigns each of the twelve houses in the birth chart to a specific region of the body. For example, the 1st House is associated with the head, the 5th House with the stomach, and the 12th House with the feet. If a particular house is afflicted or weakened, it may suggest potential health issues related to that area of the body.
Additionally, each of the nine planets used in Jyotish corresponds to specific physiological functions. Venus, for example, is linked with the reproductive system, while Saturn is associated with bones and joints. Therefore, difficulties involving a particular planet may indicate health concerns connected with the bodily systems it governs.
Combining these principles, a Vedic Astrologer might identify a tendency toward joint or bone problems affecting the feet if a problematic Saturn were located in the 12th House. However, before drawing such conclusions, the astrologer would also need to consider factors such as the overall strength of the chart, the ascendant, and other supporting influences.
Because Jyotish employs sophisticated predictive techniques, it can also indicate periods when particular health issues are more likely to arise. For instance, during the seven-year period associated with a problematic Ketu (the Moon’s south node), there may be a greater probability of illnesses that are difficult to diagnose, particularly in the area of the body governed by the house occupied by Ketu.
At a more advanced level, Jyotish combined with extensive Ayurvedic knowledge can offer considerably deeper diagnostic insights, preventive guidance, and remedial recommendations. Unfortunately, very few practitioners possess the level of expertise required in both disciplines—typically involving degree-level study in Jyotish and Ayurveda. One notable authority in this field is Dr. D. Frawley [2], who has written what is widely regarded as the definitive work on the subject.
Q. Does Jyotish use anything similar to the ‘birthstones’ found in Western Astrology?
A. First of all, it is worth noting that the modern concept of ‘birthstones’—wearing specific gemstones according to one’s birth month—has far more to do with the jewellery trade [3] than with astrology, whether Western or Vedic.
That said, the belief that gemstones can act as talismans has existed throughout much of human history. As far back as the 5th century, St. Jerome is believed to have linked certain gemstones with particular astrological signs [4]. As a result, the idea of wearing gemstones for astrological purposes is deeply rooted within our collective consciousness.
Jyotish does indeed recommend gemstones as one of many possible remedial measures intended to help balance planetary energies within the birth chart. However, this requires careful analysis of the individual chart, with particular attention paid to the ascendant.
Not every gemstone is suitable for every person. Some stones may be highly beneficial for one individual while proving unfavourable for another. The suitability of a gemstone depends entirely upon the unique planetary configuration within the birth chart. Consequently, although expensive and often given as symbols of affection, it is not always true that “diamonds are a girl’s best friend”!
